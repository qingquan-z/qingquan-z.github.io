Aim

Games are an ideal domain for studying computational intelligence (CI) because they provide affordable, competitive, dynamic, and reproducible environments for testing search algorithms, pattern-based evaluation methods, and learning approaches. They range from simple problems for developing algorithms to complex challenges that push them to their limits. CI can also improve the design and development of computer games, tabletop games, board games, and puzzles. Large language models (LLMs) also can help prototype game mechanics, generate content, and support playtesting, while also enabling game characters to respond to players during gameplay. This special session brings together researchers and practitioners to share advances in computational intelligence for games.

Scope

In general, papers are welcome that consider all kinds of applications of methods (evolutionary computation, supervised learning, unsupervised learning, fuzzy systems, game-tree search, rolling horizon algorithms, MCTS, open-ended learning etc.) to games (card games, board games, mathematical games, action games, strategy games, role-playing games, arcade games, serious games, etc.).

Examples include but are not limited to

  • Adaptation in games
  • Automatic game testing
  • Coevolution in games
  • Dynamic difficulty in games
  • Games as testbeds for AI
  • Imitating human players
  • Learning to play games
  • Multi-agent and multi-strategy learning
  • Player/opponent modelling
  • Affective game computing
  • Procedural content generation
  • CI for serious games (e.g., games for health, education or training)
  • Results of game-based CI and open competitions
  • Game and puzzle design with computational intelligence
  • Representation of knowledge in player agents
  • Representation learning and computer vision in games
  • Quality-diversity and open-ended search in games
  • LLMs and games (e.g., game design, content generation, playtesting, and player interaction with game characters)

About the organisers

Qingquan Zhang

Qingquan Zhang is currently a Ph.D. student in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech), Shenzhen, China. He received his B.S. degree in Intelligent Science and Technology from Xidian University, Xi’an, China, in 2019 and his M.S. degree in Computer Science and Technology from SUSTech in 2022. His research interests include procedural content generation, fairness in machine learning, and multi-objective learning. He is a Member of the IEEE.

Jialin Liu

Jialin Liu is currently an Associate Professor at the School of Data Science of Lingnan University, Hong Kong SAR, China. She received her Ph.D. from the Université Paris-Saclay in 2016, M.Sc. degree from the École Polytechnique and the Université Paris-Sud in 2013, Diplôme d'Ingénieur from the Polytech'Paris-Sud in 2012, and B.E. degree from the Huazhong University of Science and Technology in 2010. Her research interests include artificial intelligence in games, evolutionary computation, and fair machine learning. She is an Associate Editor of IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation, IEEE Transactions on Artificial Intelligence, IEEE Transactions on Games and Knowledge-Based Systems.

Huanhuan Chen

Huanhuan Chen is a Professor in the School of Computer Science and Technology at the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC), Hefei, China, and a Fellow of the IEEE. He received his B.Sc. degree from USTC in 2004 and his Ph.D. from the University of Birmingham, UK, in 2008. His research interests include neural networks, machine learning, and causal learning. He has served as a guest editor for the IEEE Transactions on Games special issue on Multi-Modal Content Generation for Games and as an associate editor for IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems and IEEE Transactions on Emerging Topics in Computational Intelligence.

Georgios N. Yannakakis

Georgios N. Yannakakis is a Professor at the Institute of Digital Games, University of Malta, and a co-founder of humanfeedback.ai and modl.ai. He does research at the crossroads of artificial intelligence, computational creativity, affective computing, advanced game technology, and human–computer interaction. He has published more than 450 papers in the aforementioned fields, and his work has been cited broadly. He served as Editor-in-Chief of IEEE Transactions on Games from 2022 to 2025 and previously served as an Associate Editor of IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation. He is an IEEE Fellow.
